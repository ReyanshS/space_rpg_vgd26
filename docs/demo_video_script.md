# STAR CIRCUIT: demo video script

TSA Video Game Design 2026-27. **Running time: 4:45** (the limit is 5:00).

The TSA guide says the video has to show three things. Here's where each one is:

| TSA requirement | Section |
| :--- | :--- |
| The tutorial | 3 (Level 1, rooms 1 to 3) |
| A level being played | 5 (Level 2, Main Deck, played by hand) |
| The code and the engine | 7 (Godot editor, `level.gd`, the solver, judge mode) |

**How to read this script**

- **Narration** is what the narrator reads aloud. It's timed at about 150 words a minute. Each
  section has fewer words than its time allows on purpose, so there's room for the game's own
  sounds (a door opening, the lights coming on) to be heard between lines.
- **On screen** says what to record and what to do while recording.
- **Captions** are short text overlays added when you edit the video.
- Shot numbers (like **S4**) point to the shot list at the end.

## 3. The tutorial (00:40 to 01:40)

**Narration** (126 words)

> Level one, the Engineering Deck, is our tutorial. Each room teaches one new idea.
>
> Stand on the yellow start pad and press E to grab the wire. A timer starts, and the lights over
> your head show the seconds left. Carry the wire to the blue goal pad. The room powers up, the
> lights come on, and the door opens.
>
> The wire is always pulled tight in a straight line, so it can't go through crates or walls. Touch an orange node and the wire wraps around it, and you get a few extra
> seconds.
>
> Room three adds pillars. Walk past one and the wire bends around it, like a rope around a post.
> Messed up? Press R to try again, or H for a hint.

**On screen**

- **S3, Engineering Bay (room 1)**: walk onto the yellow START pad (the arrow points at it).
  Press E. Hold on the timer LEDs over the astronaut's head for a second. Walk to the blue GOAL
  pad. Let the moment play out: the conduit fills, the ceiling lights switch on, the door slides
  open.
- **S4, Storage (room 2)**: grab the wire, walk into the crates on purpose so the wire refuses
  to pass through them (this shows the straight line rule), then touch the orange NODE so the
  wire wraps round it. Finish at the GOAL.
- **S5, Conduit Hall (room 3)**: walk past a pillar so the wire visibly wraps round it, then
  carry on round the room to the GOAL.
- During "Press R to try again, or H for a hint": **S6**, press H in a room so the yellow floor
  arrows of the hint route appear.

**Captions**

- `E / Space: grab the wire` (as he grabs it)
- `Move: W A S D or arrow keys`
- `NODE: wraps the wire and adds time` (on the node)
- `PILLAR: the wire wraps round it` (on the pillar)
- `R: restart the room   H: hint` (bottom of the screen)

**On screen**

- **S9**: the lights-on cutscene at the end of Level 1: film bars, the camera gliding back

- **S10**: the results screen (time, cells, retries, stars), then press T to open the skill tree
  and unlock one skill so it turns green.

**Captions**

- `Push crates to short out red tiles`
- `The clamp: make a corner anywhere there's a socket`
- `RPG: 3 skill branches (Wire, Suit, Life Support)` (on the skill tree)

## 5. Playing Level 2: the Main Deck (02:15 to 03:05)

This is the "level being played" the TSA guide asks for. Play it yourself, by hand, not with
judge mode.

**Narration** (101 words)

> Now let's play level two, the Main Deck. On the bridge, the rules are the same, but the room
> is harder. Then we step into the airlock. The air vents out, the outer door opens, and we're
> outside on the hull.
>
> Out here, watch your oxygen. There's no floor between the hull plates, so you walk off the
> edge and drift across to the next one. The wire can stretch across open space, but floating
> junk blocks it, and sparking tiles still shock you. And every power cell you saved in level
> one gives your suit five more seconds of air.

**On screen**

- **S11, The Bridge**: one clean run from grabbing the wire to the GOAL. Fast cuts are fine.
- **S12, Airlock**: step on the airlock pad. Keep the whole thing: the door behind closes, the
  air vents, the outer door opens, the space music starts and the O2 bar appears.
- **S13, Outer Hull**: walk off a hull edge and drift across to the next plate. Then show one
  room of the spacewalk **from grab to goal with no cuts**, so judges can see a full puzzle
  played.
- Show the O2 bar (bottom right) when the narration says "oxygen".
- If you have a good take, include one fail: step on a sparking tile, get the zap, and walk back
  to the START. It shows the game is fair about mistakes.

**Captions**

- `Spacewalk: watch your O2`
- `Walk off an edge to drift to the next plate`
- `Power cells = more oxygen later`

## 7. The code and the engine (03:25 to 04:30)

**Narration** (144 words)

> We built Star Circuit in the Godot engine, using GDScript. We don't type our levels out in
> code. We paint them. Every tile has a type, like wall, node or goal, and the game builds its
> rules from the map. Paint a node, and it comes alive right in the editor.
>
> Our main script, level dot G D, runs the rules. This function checks every step, so the wire
> stays one straight line that never cuts through a wall. The astronaut is a state machine: free,
> carrying the wire, locked, or drifting.
>
> Our Python solver finds every route through a room, so we know each puzzle can be beaten in
> time. Judge mode replays those routes, so PIP can play any room for you. We used Claude, an AI
> assistant, to help write code and tools. Our AI reflection explains exactly what it did.

**On screen**

- **S16, Godot editor**: open `Levels/level_1.tscn`, click the `Layout` TileMapLayer, and paint
  an orange node tile onto the floor. The live preview makes it glow and animate straight away.
  Zoom out a little so the room labels (number, name, START time) show too.
- **S17**: click the level's root node and scroll the Inspector to the Rules group
  (`wire_time`, `cell_bonus`), so it's clear the numbers are settings, not code.
- **S18, `Scripts/level.gd`**: scroll to `func wire_reaches(pos: Vector2) -> bool:` (about line
  1737). Highlight the function while the narration says "checks every step".
- **S19, `Scripts/player.gd`**: show `enum State { FREE, WIRED, LOCKED, DRIFT }` (line 26).
- **S20, terminal**: run `python tools/solver2.py Levels/level_1.tscn` and scroll through the
  routes and their "lowest timer" lines. (It takes about 80 seconds on Level 1 because of the
  crates: record it once and speed the clip up.)
- **S21, judge mode**: turn on JUDGE MODE on the title screen (top left), pick a level, press R
  in a room and let PIP's autopilot solve it.

**Captions**

- `Engine: Godot 4 · Language: GDScript`
- `Levels are painted: each tile has a "kind"` (S16)
- `level.gd: the rules` (S18)
- `player.gd: a state machine` (S19)
- `Python solver: checks every puzzle can be solved` (S20)
- `Judge mode: PIP's autopilot` (S21, keep it on screen the whole clip, so nobody thinks it's a
  person playing)

**Recording tips**

- For clean takes of one room, run the game from the Godot editor and use the dev menu (F1 or
  the ` key) to jump straight to any room. It only exists in editor builds, so don't show it in
  the video.
- Gameplay in sections 3 to 6 should be played by a person. Only S21 uses the autopilot, and its
  caption says so.
- If the web build is ready, record the gameplay shots in a browser: it's how the judges will
  play it.

## Check before you record

These came up while writing the script. Sort them out so the video and the portfolio agree.

1. **Team ID**: the portfolio's title page says 2036773, but the AI reflection form says
   2010748. Put the right one in the captions.
2. **Target audience**: the portfolio says 2nd to 3rd graders, while `docs/how_to_play.md` says
   10 and up. The narration avoids naming an age, but the portfolio should pick one.
3. **AI reflection**: the portfolio says AI was never asked to come up with game mechanics or
   make artwork, and that all level designs are the team's. `CLAUDE.md` records more than that
   (for example: Claude designed Level 2's and Level 3's layouts and the core loop rule, drew
   the side walls, pillars, planet and nebula, made the sound effects, and wrote the solver).
   The video only says Claude helped with code and tools and points to the reflection, so update
   the reflection to match `CLAUDE.md` before you submit, or judges may notice the gap.
4. **Coolant Pumps**: `CLAUDE.md` says the solver finds no solution there since the last crate
   edit. Play it before recording Level 1, and don't film it until it's fixed.
5. **Controls in the portfolio**: it lists F1 for dev mode, but the dev menu only exists when
   the game runs from the Godot editor, so judges playing the web build can't use it. Leave it
   out of the video and the controls page. Q only works while the pause menu is open (Esc, then
   Q), so say that in the portfolio too. It's also missing T (skill tree).
